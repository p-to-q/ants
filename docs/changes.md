# Changes

Append-only, newest first, only durable changes. Three lines each. `Forecloses:` names the door the change shuts.

## 2026-10-19 · Existing series and history are not first-run installations
Changed: bare `ants` shares the viewer's ledger discovery and recognizes a retained history index even after active ledgers move; regression tests cover series-only, archive-only, damaged-cache and truly empty state.
Why: a deployed server without an onboarding config incorrectly offered installation despite already running a series.
Forecloses: misleading dataset-download prompts after upgrades or migrations, writing configuration just to read history, and treating a damaged cache as a new installation.

## 2026-10-19 · Past keeps previous research rounds readable
Changed: a final read-only terminal page groups verified frozen bundles by round, with cached descriptive results, coverage, epoch, parameters and recorded backup locations; a bounded, atomic index is built explicitly; snapshots omit comparative metrics by default. Series workdirs are discovered one level below the series root.
Why: a growing experiment history needs a useful overview without rescanning old ledgers or presenting operational checkpoints as scientific conclusions.
Forecloses: cross-epoch rankings, best-seed cherry picking, silent missing-run imputation, live-backup claims from cached receipts, and restore or deletion actions hidden in a history page.

## 2026-10-19 · Preflight keeps prefix-sharing arms separate (post-release operations fix)
Changed: a seed workdir matches its arm only when the entire suffix after `_s` is numeric; adds regressions for plain and seeded `portfolio` / `portfolio_screen` workdirs and malformed suffixes. Evaluator files, contracts, data, budgets and pinned arm hashes are unchanged.
Why: the first E43a deployment showed that `portfolio_screen_s1` was also assigned to `portfolio`, producing a false definition-mismatch failure despite correct ledger identities.
Forecloses: treating a shared directory-name prefix as ownership of a ledger, or hiding a real mismatch by weakening definition checks. Published v0.6.2 tags and artifacts are not rewritten.

## 2026-10-19 · Deployment preflight and recoverable E43 operations (0.6.2)
Changed: adds a read-only `ants preflight` for pinned server arms, ledgers, disk headroom and runtime prerequisites; centralizes arm-definition hashing so preflight, experiment checks and running campaigns agree; hardens deployment bundles, backup verification, migration safety (including distinguishing a restored ledger from an older same-schema rollback copy by its event-chain endpoint), optional-operator restarts and series execution; ensures preflight never acquires an arm's run lock; adds the E43a registry/runner and a working arm-aware post-deploy verification path.
Why: deployment must establish what is running and what is preserved before changing a host; a recovery path is part of the experiment, not an afterthought.
Forecloses: installing over an unverified ledger, treating a missing optional operator as a new arm definition, starting a series against a different epoch, or calling a bundle verified before its contents and chain have been checked.

## 2026-10-17 · Outside readings, the research memo, and four interface bugs (0.6.1)
Changed: (1) the interface's catalog lookup (`home.find_catalog`; the evaluator's `data.locate_catalog` keeps its behaviour because it is part of the epoch hash, and a test pins the fingerprint) no longer fails with `PermissionError` when a parent of the working directory cannot be entered by the service user; Health and `ants doctor` now say whether the catalog and the development datasets can be read by this user. (2) Every reader that shows or ranks results now reads the arm's current epoch only (`History.for_arm`): the interface, `ants top` and `best`, the workspace export and the agent's tools; before, they ingested every epoch. (3) The leaderboard is cumulative: cap N lists every model of at most N parameters (`leaderboard(cumulative=False)` keeps the registry's bins). (4) The System page lists the model calls of every ledger with their total, says that the daily cap applies to each ledger separately and where the cap comes from, and says how many trials restoring the newest backup would lose. Documentation: the gaps named by outside readers (no operating-system isolation, the portfolio not shown better than simple search, a parameter count that is not a deployment cost, an unwired final claim, an empty benchmark, early reliability evidence only, figures that reproduce while results do not, a hash chain that is not yet a fully trusted ledger, a premise already supported by SearchCast and by Toner and Darlow) are in the roadmap and `SECURITY.md`, one step each; `docs/research/search-landscape.md` records the first reading of the literature on search over a landscape; the roadmap states the target precisely (speed, quality, probability and evidence within a fixed budget), fixes the next work in three stages with stop rules, and adds steps 24 to 40 (results bundle, OS isolation, measured deployment cost, the final claim, soak evidence, ledger audit, research agents with a programme and a granted budget, the four reported quantities, islands, search mechanisms as route properties, space expansion as a separate action, a decision log, evaluation of a new policy, a budget for tuning the search, training-lifecycle coverage, a model-call budget, the reward ladder) and research items R7 to R14. `docs/research/ledger-first-look.md` and `scripts/ledger_first_look.py` record a first look at the three server ledgers (about 86 000 trials): the top of each arm is one lineage, the second deep stage repeats the first at Spearman 0.995 or more and costs 28 to 39% of the CPU, the first stage predicts the second at 0.12 to 0.26 among promoted trials, and a surrogate fitted on early trials ranks later ones at 0.93 to 0.98; the roadmap adds steps 42 to 45 (an unselected first-stage sample, a learned screen, independent roots, an epoch-3 package) and records the readings R10 to R13 in the memo. It records what an arm, a portfolio and an operator are, what Jev is and that nothing calls it, and the measured finding that the two model arms exhaust their daily call cap with many failed calls. Optuna becomes the provider of the reference search arms (TPE with multivariate and grouped sampling, GP, CMA-ES, NSGA-II, successive halving and Hyperband pruners) under a fairness rule, and the existing `tpe` arm is documented as a weak floor (default univariate sampler, first-stage values only). The shared layer between arms is specified without a language model (evaluation cache, mutation-effect atlas from lineage edits, shared surrogate and cost model, failure map, refutation register, region claims), with rules against false findings. A self-modifying agent method waits for a held-out dataset group and space expansion waits for operating-system isolation. The rule for everything that adapts is written down: it may change how the next round searches and never what counts as success.
Why: each of these was a correct observation; a statement that is not made is cheaper than one that is withdrawn, and a display that mixes epochs or bins is a wrong statement made on every refresh.
Forecloses: reading the nine operators as an ecology before the leave-one-out ablation says so, ranking arms on one seed each, letting a mined pattern count before trials made after it support it, reporting a win over 'Optuna TPE' that used the plain default configuration, reading results of other epochs in any display, a leaderboard label that means something other than its words, a search policy or an agent that moves the finish line, an agent that spends its own budget or picks its own depth, editing an evaluator file for any reason other than a planned epoch, putting `rel_mse` beside a published MSE, presenting closed-form tiny models as a finding first made here, building communication between investigators before one investigator is shown to beat the memoryless agent, claiming a new policy better from a replay of recorded trials alone, tuning the search policy on confirm results, reading the search as a shortest-path problem, calling a result near-optimal when the optimum is unknown, letting an agent change the space and the judge in one action, calling the boundary a sandbox, the chain a trusted ledger, the parameter cap a deployment result or the portfolio a better optimiser before the step that would show it.

## 2026-10-13 · Versions, upgrades and continuity (0.6.0)
Changed: every start of the loop writes a `daemon_started` event (ants version, epoch, python, arm definition and its hash); `ants epochs` lists the epochs of a ledger with counts, dates and versions; `ants doctor` warns when an arm's definition changed inside one ledger; the server deploy takes a verified backup of every ledger first, stops if one fails and appends the version and package hash to `/var/lib/ants/DEPLOY.log`; `docs/upgrading.md` states what an upgrade never touches, what each kind of change does to a running search, the compatibility promises, how to go back and five rules for changing the harness without losing results. The interface: the word and the name sit beside the ant with air under the picture, a live line says how many searches are doing well, the refresh interval adapts to a slow ledger.
Why: moving the first three arms by hand was the right call once; the next change must not depend on anyone remembering to.
Forecloses: an upgrade that replaces or deletes a workdir; reusing an arm name for a different definition; an epoch change that nobody can see afterwards.

## 2026-10-13 · Why the one-shot model operator produced nothing (0.5.1)
Changed: the new `proposer_yield` events showed it within minutes: every proposal the one-shot operator received (6 of 6, five calls in a row) was rejected by the strict spec validator, because the model returned only the levers it wanted to change and the validator demands all keys. The operator now fills left-out levers with their defaults, as the agent operator already did. The first epoch's `portfolio_llm` was therefore never an LLM arm, and not only because the bandit starved it.
Why: a counter that lives in memory could not say it; a recorded one did on the first look.
Forecloses: reading any `portfolio_llm` result from before 0.5.1 as evidence about language models; strict-only validation of model output without a recorded reject reason.

## 2026-10-13 · Epoch 2 and the second version of the interface (0.5.0)
Changed: from the analysis of the first eight hours of three searches: the contract `contracts/tinyts_real.toml` promotes per size cap (`promote_by_cap`) so the 100 and 300 caps reach the deeper stages (it changes the contract hash, so it is a new epoch); the language-model operators (`llm`, `agent`) are guaranteed a fifth of every batch (`portfolio.LLM_FLOOR`), because the bandit had starved the one-shot operator to two evaluated trials in eight hours; every model call writes a `proposer_yield` event (proposed, invalid, duplicate, accepted). The interface: plain-language status per search (`tui/vibe.py`), live animation with reduced-motion and slow-link modes (`tui/anim.py`), a braille chart, heat strips, stacked stage bars, a log-scale lollipop, a ribbon of trials, gauges and a 7-day backup axis; keys `p` (pause or resume), `h` (hint), `[` `]` (switch search), space (freeze), `g` `G`; the home screen carries the one-line description and a short introduction. `docs/tui.md` is now the manual; `docs/roadmap.md` records the converged plan; documentation fixes from an independent audit (CLI flags and environment variables, command counts, stale "not run" statements, question numbering). The logo files are unchanged.
Why: the first results were mostly a statement about the protocol, not about the searches (an operator that never ran, caps that were never searched); fixing them before the comparison is cheaper than explaining them after.
Forecloses: pooling results from before and after the epoch change; reading the three arms' first eight hours as a comparison of proposers; an interface that shows only numbers.

## 2026-10-13 · Second review: the agent's shared connection, the interface's hard edges (0.4.1)
Changed: an independent reviewer reproduced that the agent operator's pool threads read through the campaign's own SQLite connection, which stranded trials and crashed loop steps (the chain stayed valid; the live `portfolio_agent` arm ran with this for about two hours before the fix, with no failures recorded). Jobs now read through their own read-only connection and `History`; tool failures of any kind are answers to the model and `agent_tool` events (tool names cleaned and clamped); the `trials.out_dir` column reads as NULL and `sqlite_sequence` is denied for the agent; `show`, `diff` and the parent are scoped to the arm; hostile ids are refused. The interface: a ledger that cannot be opened is a row on its screen; data for another screen is never handed to a view; strings a gym wrote are cleaned before they reach the screen; the command line no longer offers `top`, `export`, `workspace`, keeps the quotes in a hint, survives a resize and Ctrl-C; the pager scrolls; a viewer falls back to an immutable read when the directory is read-only and no write-ahead log exists. Also: LLM cap counts calls in flight and rejects a negative setting; `update.py` survives odd caches and config; workspace files are written through unpredictable temp names.
Why: the first reviewer of the interface and the agent found what tests written by the author had not: a thread-safety fault and a dozen ways a terminal program can fall over.
Forecloses: a pool thread sharing the loop's database connection; trusting that tool errors have a fixed type; a view fed data loaded for another screen.

## 2026-10-13 · The terminal interface
Changed: plain `ants` on a terminal opens a curses interface (home with the mark and one line per running search, overview, leaderboard per cap, trials with detail, arms side by side, events, health, data, system) instead of the line shell, which remains for pipes and dumb terminals; `ants ui` and `ants home --screen …` (one screen printed) are explicit; the viewer opens the ledger with `Ledger.open_readonly`.
Why: a person should be able to SSH to the machine and look at the search, its ranking, its failures and its health without asking anyone to run commands.
Forecloses: a viewer that takes write locks on a live ledger; screens that compute their own numbers; starting, restoring or installing from inside the interface.

## 2026-10-13 · An operator that reads the ledger (from the Meta-Harness study)
Changed: `agent` and `portfolio_agent` arms: the LLM operator may call five read-only tools over the ledger (`sql`, `show`, `diff`, `best`, `failures`, at most six per batch, `sql` with the tables of sealed looks, events, endpoints and artifacts denied) before it proposes specs, states a hypothesis and an axis (mechanism or parameter) and may name a parent for lineage; every call is a chained `agent_tool` event. `ants workspace` writes the ledger as plain files with a skill file; `ants diff` compares two trials. `docs/research/meta-harness.md` records the paper, its code, the neighbouring and downstream work as read in the originals, and the mapping to ants.
Why: the one-shot operator conditions on a window of best specs, the regime in which the paper found summaries add nothing; ants already holds the full experience in the ledger and lacked a proposer that reads it.
Also: `ants run` exited the whole process after a normal finish whenever any thread was alive, which under pytest silently ended the test run midway with status 0 (the suite had been stopping early); it now does so only when the LLM client's workers exist. Version 0.3.0, units sized for three arms (66% CPU, 650M each).
Forecloses: giving the proposer a shell or file access (the tool set is closed, the evaluator stays out of reach by construction, not instruction); an unlogged read; reading the sealed-look records; claiming a benefit before the equal-budget comparison of open.md question 9.

## 2026-10-13 · Review of the system layer: 11 findings fixed, update check, 0.2.0
Changed: an independent reviewer broke the first version of backup, restore, service, fetch and the versioned ledger. Fixed: backup anchors are readable by `verify_anchor_file`; restore moves a stale write-ahead log aside, never overwrites an earlier moved-aside ledger, refuses a snapshot older than the ledger it would replace or taken from another ledger (`--allow-different`) and writes a `restored` event; backups get exclusive names, leave nothing behind on failure, skip junk sidecars and are ordered by creation time; default backup directories include a hash of the workdir; the unit file escapes `%`, `$` and backslashes; a heartbeat thread covers the long blocking model calls (dream, refill) for at most 15 minutes so the 300 s watchdog does not restart a healthy loop; `ants onboard` keeps earlier choices and a corrupt `config.toml` is reported; a ledger from a newer ants is refused before any write, and the first migration keeps a full copy; `ants sql` caps value sizes; dataset fetching refuses unsafe names and plain-http redirects and deletes partial downloads; LLM spend is capped per rolling day (`ANTS_LLM_MAX_CALLS_PER_DAY`, default 1500). Added `ants update` and a quiet once-a-day check; units for janitor, health and backup are now per arm (`@<arm>`), and the campaign unit is `Type=notify` with a watchdog; version 0.2.0.
Why: a resident system is judged by what it does on the day something goes wrong; each of these was reproduced, not guessed.
Forecloses: restoring an older snapshot over a newer ledger without saying so; an update that happens inside a running daemon; a watchdog shorter than the longest blocking call; units that name one arm.

## 2026-10-13 · A resident system: onboard, service, backup, sql, versioned ledger
Changed: `ants onboard` (home directory, datasets from `data/sources.toml` checked against the pinned manifest, `config.toml`), `ants service install` (systemd user unit with `Type=notify` and a watchdog heartbeat sent by the loop itself, launchd agent, daily backup timer), `ants backup/backups/restore` (SQLite online backup, kept only if integrity and chain verify, sidecar and `anchors.jsonl`, restore moves the old file aside), `ants sql` (read-only authorizer, time and row caps), `ants data fetch`, plain `ants` offers setup on the first run; the ledger carries `PRAGMA user_version` with forward-only migrations and refuses a file written by a newer ants; `ants doctor` adds integrity, WAL size, network filesystem and backup age.
Why: one command should wake it, keep it alive and recoverable, and make its data readable without a second tool. The pattern is the one OpenClaw, Pi, NanoClaw, TinyClaw and nanobot share (home directory plus one config, a service installer, a status probe); none of them verifies a backup or versions its store, which is where a long-lived ledger needs more.
Forecloses: copying the live ledger file as a backup; a service that restarts a hung loop only by timeout; a ledger opened by an older ants than wrote it; downloading datasets without a pinned hash or over plain http.

## 2026-10-13 · Name, one line, and what the README no longer stresses
Changed: ants now reads as **Autonomous Navigation of Tiny-model Space**; the one line is "A small autonomous research loop: it starts on one cheap machine and scales with the compute you give it." The slogan "Agents Never Touch Scoring", "CPU-only", "evidence ledger" and "hard parameter budgets" are gone from the name, the banner, the repository description and the first paragraph; the architecture figure no longer draws the ledger. Small and cheap describe where ants starts, not what it is limited to.
Why: the ledger is a necessary part, not a selling point; a parameter cap describes the first domain, not the project; nano-style projects are defined by starting small and growing.
Forecloses: describing ants by its cap or its ledger; using the old slogan as a name or tagline.

## 2026-10-13 · Red team: sealed credits, registry proof, roles, anchors
Changed: sealed looks are scoped per (gym, epoch, sealed set) with a lifetime cap, one look per freeze hash, counted from the hash chain as well as the table, and closed by exactly one result; `Registry` climbs only with recorded evidence (`confirm`, `challenge` on identical windows with online FDR, a sealed result for the champion); `decide_promotion` fails closed on cherry-picked or malformed evidence; roles are enforced in `Catalog.load` when `ANTS_ALLOWED_ROLES` is set (the campaign sets `dev`, `gate.seal` sets `dev,confirm,sealed`) and surrogates inherit the role of sealed/confirm bases; catalog edits are validated; ledger anchors detect tail truncation and rewrites; `frontier`, `seen_keys` and `freeze_best` use the current epoch only; `gmre` and the paired statistics raise on malformed input.
Why: the freeze-then-seal design was sound but its bookkeeping could be bypassed (refunded credits, forged results, bare `transition()`, a challenger that dropped the families it loses on, an unchecked sealed dataset argument).
Forecloses: counting sealed looks from a mutable table alone; promoting a model without recorded evidence; treating the role firewall as closed: it is opt-in per process, the default is open, and the registry, `gate.seal` and anchors have no production caller.

## 2026-10-13 · Security hardening: AST patch gate, transport, contract, deploy
Changed: the maintainer gate parses code (AST before/after, imports allow-list, frozen I/O code) instead of matching text, and its sandbox runs with a minimal environment, rlimits and a nonce-protected golden replay; LLM and Jev transport is https-only with no redirects and bounded, strict reads; `extract_json` and `Space.validate` cannot hang or raise foreign errors; the contract loader validates every field; terminal output goes through `textsafe.clean_text`; the board escapes everything, carries a CSP and `ants serve` uses a loopback-only, Host-checked server; packaging scans the staging tree and the tarball for secrets; the units were renamed to `ants*`, no longer load `llm.env` into the environment and add sandboxing options.
Why: reproduced bypasses of the text deny-list, an API key forwarded on redirects, unbounded reads, unsanitised terminal and HTML output, and an installer that referenced files that did not exist.
Forecloses: a raw-text deny-list as the maintainer's only gate; redirects on calls that carry a key; describing the AST gate as a sandbox (it is static analysis; the TinyTS evaluator is still on the maintainer's allow list); claiming the units are proven (they were never started under systemd).

## 2026-10-13 · Chaos: crash, clock, disk and shutdown paths
Changed: one `ants run` per workdir (`flock`, second instance exits 3); SIGTERM/SIGINT stop a running trial within seconds (0.25 s polling, SIGTERM, 3 s grace, SIGKILL) and requeue it; a failed `COMMIT` no longer wedges the connection; transient SQLite/OS errors are retried instead of killing the daemon; event timestamps never go backwards and the breaker and timeouts use the monotonic clock; `metrics.json` is read without blocking, size-capped and must be a flat dict of finite numbers; escapees that left the process group are killed by a per-trial token; archive retries mismatches and retires vanished files; the janitor re-checks every delete condition on a fresh row; relative `--workdir` works; the epoch is stamped at claim.
Why: reproduced double evaluation by two daemons, lost evaluations on a locked database, a hung supervisor on a FIFO named `metrics.json`, a stuck archive window and deletions based on a stale plan.
Forecloses: assuming one process per workdir without the lock; reading `metrics.json` with `read_bytes()`; trusting a plan older than the delete call. Still open: finish and promotion are not atomic, and a daemon `kill -9` outside systemd leaves its gym running.

## 2026-10-13 · Independent audit: 36 findings, release blocked until fixed
Changed: an isolated reviewer audited a snapshot (4 blocker, 9 high, 19 medium, 4 low). Fixed so far: history readers saw only the first 20 000 trials; malformed model JSON could crash `dream` and leak proposer slots; "standard library only" now describes the search loop (statistics, data and gyms need numpy) and a test enforces it; no LICENSE; package name collision. In progress: systemd file-size limit on the ledger, relative `--workdir`, epoch coverage, installer deleting state, maintainer patch gate, janitor delete-time checks.
Why: the thesis (agents never touch scoring) is only as strong as the weakest unchecked claim, and several README claims were not supported by code.
Forecloses: shipping before the blockers are closed; describing the core as "stdlib only" without qualification.

## 2026-10-13 · Named ants; Apache-2.0
Changed: the project is **ants**, read as **Agents Never Touch Scoring**; package `ants-lab`, module `antslab`, command `ants`; code licensed Apache-2.0.
Why: a short name for a thing that works by many small agents leaving a trail; Apache-2.0 is the p-to-q default and gives an explicit patent grant.
Forecloses: publishing under the bare name `ants` on npm or PyPI (taken); a copyleft licence for the code.

## 2026-10-13 · Private design thinking stays out of the repository
Changed: design notes, literature sweeps and idea logs live in `internal/` and are excluded by `.gitignore` and `scripts/pack_public.sh`.
Why: the public record should be code, contracts, evidence and the principles that are enforced; the rest is working memory.
Forecloses: citing private notes as justification inside public docs.

## 2026-10-12 · Hard cap as a constraint, rolling-origin validation on real data
Changed: parameter caps gate candidates instead of entering the objective; stages S1/S2 use expanding-window folds across several families.
Why: a single fixed validation segment reversed model rankings on ETTh1; a penalty term lets over-cap models lead.
Forecloses: a scalar "accuracy minus lambda × size" headline.
